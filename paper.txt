Abstract—Signing more contracts with complex terms and
agreements is leading to more conflicts among multiple docu-
ments and signatories, as lawyers face difficult contracts with
varying parties, multiple documents will be involved in a trans-
action, and there will be multiple filings. For this reason, the
following paper proposes the conflict resolution system based on
the Multiple Evidence and Multiple Defendants principles. The
AI system processes the data in four steps: Data Ingestion and
Pre-processing; Entity Extraction using Deep Learning; Evidence
Identification and Conflict Detection; Explanation Generation.
Natural Language Processing is applied to a set of∼11,000 legal
documents. SpaCy is used for tokenization, tagging of the BIO
sequences, and vectorization of the data. A BiLSTM-CRF model
is built to find important legal entities which include a defendant,
evidence, date, court, place, and event. The information that
is collected is arranged into a knowledge graph to show how
they relate to each other and claims. Three major types of
anomalies will be discovered by applying rule reasoning: possible
contradictions, missing information, and possible manipulation of
evidence.
Index Terms—AI, BiLSTM-CRF, identifying conflicts, knowl-
edge graphs, analyzing legal documents, recognizing named
entities, and processing natural languages.
I. INTRODUCTION
The legal profession is one that deals with a great deal of
written information, such as court orders, statutory statements,
witness testimony, investigation documents, evidence, forensic
documents, trial records, and legal documents. In complex
cases, and particularly when it is necessary to establish con-
nections between defendants based on evidence and statements
from multiple documents, lawyers must access information
from several documents. This is often hard to do where there
are many defendants and multiple evidence sources involved
in the case.
It may take a great deal of time to do this work manually
and can also be challenging to ensure accuracy. The difficulty
is exacerbated further if the data gathered from the different
sources is not consistent or even contradictory. Legal profes-
sionals need to pay significant attention and effort to review
many documents and come up with these inconsistencies.
Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML)
have lately presented new opportunities for the analysis oflegal documents. There are several applications of AI in legal
document analysis already. There have been some previous
studies on classification of documents, summarization of legal
documents, identification of legal entities, and predicting the
outcome of legal cases. Natural Language Processing (NLP)
techniques have also been applied to the task of extracting en-
tities like judges and courts, defendants and other legal persons
from legal documents. Roughly, deep learning methods, such
as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Long Short-
Term Memory (LSTM) networks have also been leveraged to
process and analyze legal information.
Most of the existing approaches, however, are limited to a
particular task and do not take into account the links between
the various pieces of evidence. Both could analyse a single
document or carry out a specific legal action, but they have
limited ability to support cases involving multiple defendants,
conflicting evidence, missing information, and contradictions
between documents.
AI-based legal systems are predominantly studied in areas
like legal judgment prediction, criminal activity prediction,
entity recognition, and document analysis in legal documents,
as presented in the literature reviewed for this project. A
crucial area of unmet need is the absence of a system that
can efficiently accommodate a large number of defendants
and evidence sources, and detect inconsistencies and gaps in
evidence that exist between them. This has led to the need for a
system that would be able to analyse data from several sources,
to look for links between the extracted data and mark potential
conflicts for further consideration by legal professionals.
The primary goal of this research is to create a framework
for conflict resolution capable of analyzing data from multiple
defendants and evidence sources. In complex legal cases,
simply collecting information is not sufficient; the information
from different sources must also be compared to determine
whether it is consistent. For instance, one defendant could
testify at a specific time and place, another defendant may
provide different information. Understanding these disparities
can aid lawyers in analyzing the evidence.
The proposed framework is to be used to process a set
of legal documents of around 11000 documents. These docu-
===== PAGE 2 =====
ments are preprocessed with various methods, including text
extraction, cleaning, tokenization, BIO sequence labelling and
vectorization. Finally, an important legal entity is identified
using BiLSTM-CRF model.
The framework is made up of 4 key modules. The first
module, Data Ingestion and Preprocessing, helps to transform
raw documents into structured data by extracting text, cleaning
it, tokenizing it and post-processing. The second module is
the Deep Learning Based Named Entity Recognition, which
is implemented based on the BiLSTM-CRF architecture to
recognize the important legal entities and relationship among
the entities in the text. In the third module, Evidence Cross-
Referencing and Conflict Detection, this information is con-
trasted between the various sources and displayed in a knowl-
edge graph to detect any inconsistencies, missing information,
and contradictions. Finally, the Explainable Insights and Re-
porting module displays the anomalies detected and explains
them, which can assist users to understand the reasons for the
anomaly that was detected.
The basic idea of the proposed framework is to combine
together information extraction, deep learning, knowledge
representation and rule-based reasoning in a single system.
The aim of the system is not to supplant lawyers nor to
make final legal judgments. Instead, it’s designed to help them
find potentially important inconsistencies and produce reports
which can be checked and confirmed via human experts.
The rest of this paper provides a literature review, method-
ology, experimental results and conclusions of the proposed
framework.
II. PROPOSEDMETHODOLOGY
The Multi-Evidence and Multi-Defendant Conflict Resolver
is an approach that involves NLP and deep learning of un-
structured legal text to extract structured information from the
text and identify conflicts/inconsistencies among evidence and
legal entities. The method is based on SpaCy, NLP processing,
BIO tagging, LegalBERT, XGBoost and Relational Graph
Convolutional Networks (R-GCN). The entire process of the
proposed methodology can be explained as follows: As can be
seen in Fig. 1, the NLP entity extraction and pre-processing,
model training, anomaly detection and conflict identification,
and the generation of structured insights can be explained.
A. NLP Entity Extraction
The first step of the methodology is the Entity Identifica-
tion process which involves the identification of significant
entities in the case documents. Understanding of relationships
and inconsistencies found in legal documents like judgments,
petitions, witness statements and text regarding evidence. In
the project, NLP processing and entity extraction are done
using SpaCy.
Through the extraction process, the system discovers legal
entities like courts, petitioners, respondents, lawyers, judges,
defendants and other entities involved with the case. They
can be abstracted and used to organize information from the
original legal document. This structured information then is
Fig. 1. Methodology Flowchart
fed to the downstream models, which are used to detect
anomalies, contradictions, and model relationship, and identify
entities.
B. Preprocessing and BIO Tagging
After the entity extraction process, the legal documents are
preprocessed for representation in an appropriate format to
be used to train the model. The project aims at correctly
linking the annotated entities with the respective tokens with
the help of the technique called tokenization and character-
span alignment.
The annotations are converted to BIO (Beginning, Inside,
Outside) tagging format. The BIO tagging technique assigns a
B-tag to the start of an entity, I-tag to tokens within the entity,
and O-tag to tokens not in the entity. Token based sequence is
important but character based alignment is important, because
some of the original annotations may contain references to
entities based on the characters position, and the model based
on sequence uses tokens.
The primary purpose of this pre-processing step is to trans-
form the annotated legal documents into a stream of tokens,
each of which includes a legal entity tag that will be used to
train the model for legal entity recognition. This also maintains
the linkage of the original text and the entity annotations.

C. Model Training Using BiLSTM-CRF
The BiLSTM-CRF (Bidirectional Long Short-Term Mem-
ory – Conditional Random Field) legal entity recognition
model receives the sequences that the BIO tags have re-
trieved. In order for the model to learn the context of words
surrounding the current word, the BiLSTM layer is actually
run backward and forwards. This is especially helpful when
working with legal texts because the words that come before
and after a given entity can affect how it is interpreted. The
CRF creates stable entity tag sequences by modeling the output
labels’ dependencies. In the preprocessed case text, the system
can identify structured legal entities using the BiLSTM and
CRF layers. At this stage, known entities are generated and
then used for relationship, conflict and anomaly analysis.
D. Conflict and Anomaly Detection
The entities and the information they contain are then used
in a conflict stage and an anomaly detection stage. The stage is
reached with a combination of three complementary methods:
LegalBERT, XGBoost and R-GCN. LegalBERT is used for
contradiction detection. The statements may be treated as
inconsistent in law if a person makes a number of statements
to the police, or if different witnesses give different ac-
counts. Legal purposes may have conflicting information from
two or more defendants, witnesses or sources of evidence.
LegalBERT provides a language model based approach to
semantically associate legal statements and identify potential
inconsistencies. XGBoost is used for anomaly detection. The
entity information and features retrieved in previous stages can
be utilized to detect any abnormal or inconsistent patterns in
the structured entity information and features. This portion of
the component can be useful for finding information that does
not match the expected pattern and may need to be investigated
further. The third is R-GCN (Relational Graph Convolutional
Network) for relationship modelling.
There may be many defendants and entities involved and
many types of evidence that connect in different ways. R-
GCN gives a graphical visualization of these relationships and
is useful for analyzing the connections between defendants,
entities, and evidence. The methodology can use these three
models for the detection of semantic contradiction, feature-
based anomaly detection and relational modeling of legal
information.
The three models are used in three distinct ways for the
analysis of legal information: R-GCN for relational modeling,
XGBoost for feature-based anomaly detection, and Legal-
BERT for semantic contradiction detection.
III. LITERATURESURVEY
Legal document analysis using Artificial Intelligence and
Natural Language Processing has increased rapidly over the
years due to its various applications such as information
extraction, legal ruling prediction, argument evaluation, and
statement impact analysis. Despite this, previous research
mainly focuses on application of artificial intelligence tech-
nology for only one type of legal task instead of combiningdifferent processes such as entity extraction, multi-defendant
analysis, evidence comparison, conflict identification, and ex-
plodable reporting. Existing works forming the basis for the
development of an integrated model are provided in current
literature.
Research done by Chen et al. [12] covered defendant-aware
label representations with the view of multi-defendant charge
prediction. Their study is directly connected with the proposed
model since it suggests that multiple defendants should be the
subject of analysis rather than treating the case as one entity.
Their work stresses the necessity to differentiate between data
related to each defendant in legal case analysis. However, the
focus of the research is related to the prediction of charges in a
case, while this paper aims at comparing evidence and claims
provided by different defendants in order to obtain conflict
clues.
According to Wang and colleagues [13], LegalReasoner is
a framework that proposes a combination of large language
models with knowledge integration to arrive at legal decisions.
The authors illustrate the application of structured legal knowl-
edge alongside language models to facilitate the complex legal
reasoning process. Similarly, Yamada et al. [17] considered the
legal judgment decision-making process based on structured
legal case details. The two works illustrate the significance
of relating legal facts and relationships, as opposed to merely
relying on textual features. However, the main goal of the
studies presented remains the prediction of court decisions.
Luo et al. [18] examined the issues of multi-party dialogue
mining to extract legal evidence from governmental speeches.
The work can be tied back to the presented framework
since specific courtroom statements can contain information
correlating to particular individuals. Using multi-party con-
versations as a source of evidence will allow to differentiate
between the voices of various players. Yet, evidence retrieval
alone cannot establish whether the information provides con-
tradictory information.
Gartner and G ¨ohlich [15] looked at detection of contradic-
tions with the assistance of formal logic and big language
models. Their research shows how logical and language-based
methods can be employed in the process of contradiction
detection. This is relevant for the conflict engine which utilizes
the power of rule-based logic as well. However, their approach
is not developed specifically for multi-defendant evidence.
Mantravadi et al. [20] discussed a multi-agent framework
for contradiction detection in legal documents. The given
technique demonstrates the efficiency of employing several co-
operating entities for dealing with complex legal information.
Still, this research goes beyond what has been discussed as it
combines legal entity extraction, division of evidence by de-
fendants, representation of extracted information in knowledge
graphs, and rule-based detection of conflict into a sequential
process.
Tariq and Woo [24] studied multimodal deepfake detection
for judicial evidence verification. This work is related to
integrity of the evidence part of the proposed system since it
demonstrates that the need for explaination is very important
===== PAGE 4 =====
when dealing with manipulated digital evidence. However,
deepfake detection deals with authenticity of the media only.
In their work, Smith et al. [25] explore how to utilize
reliable AI in proceedings by evaluating ambiguity in multi-
source evidence. They underline the significance of ambiguity
in cases where information comes from several sources of
evidence. This is relevant to the framework proposed since
conflict detection should seek inconsistent evidence instead of
immediately discarding one source as being legally invalid.
According to the system proposed, human verification will
also feature as the last stage.
In sum, all the studies analyzed indicate that there has been
a great deal of progress in the field of legal NLP, defendant-
aware modeling, knowledge-oriented reasoning, contradiction
detection, evidence verification, and explainable AI. Nonethe-
less, this progress has been addressed as separate issues.
As a result, the literature reveals a gap connected with the
systems capable of simultaneously completing many tasks
such as extracting legal entities, linking claims with several
defendants, illustrating relationships among various evidence
types, comparing contradictory evidence, and finding absent
evidence or manipulation patterns.
The table shows a summary of the relevant research that
has been done for the proposed system. Various solutions have
been used for various parts of the problem, such as defendant-
aware modelling [12], knowledge-based legal reasoning [13],
contradiction detection [15], evidence extraction [18], multi-
agent analysis [20] and explainable evidence verification [24]
as it can be seen in the literature reviewed above. While
the studies mentioned above are helpful, they still do not
provide a comprehensive way of processing various types
of legal documents and gathering the information on the
basis of the accused person, building connections between
pieces of evidence/reports, identifying conflicts, and forming
a detailed report. For this reason, the proposed system – the
Multi-Evidence and Multi-Defendant Conflict Resolver that
integrates the above-mentioned features into one analytical
process – is going to be developed.
IV. RESULTS ANDDISCUSSION
To test the usefulness of the Multi-Evidence and Multi-
Defendant Conflict Resolver, legal entity extraction, anomaly
detection, contradiction resolution and relationship modeling
techniques were developed and analyzed. The BiLSTM-CRF
technique, XGBoost method, R-GCN (Relational Graph Con-
volutional Networks) solution, and LegalBERT framework
were the four main frameworks used in the experiments. The
outcomes of the pilot phase demonstrate the components are
effective and will be ready to be incorporated into a more
advanced legal dispute resolution process.
A. BiLSTM-CRF Named Entity Recognition Results
For identifying the legal entities, we needed to train the
BiLSTM-CRF method. The key to the model’s success was the
text tokenization process, which enabled the model to detect
multiple legal entities in one text. The obtained accuracy in theclassification of tokens is 93.16% which is very impressive.
Application of the method of 5-fold cross-validation shows
that method accuracy is equal to 93.45% (±1.46%), which
indicates its effectiveness at processing the texts containing
the BIO tags. The role of the BiLSTM method is of utmost
importance since it is the legal entities that provide the
meaning of tokens, whereas the use of context helps to arrange
them correctly. The CRF layer improves the tagging method
even further thanks to the ability to take into account the
neighborhood of the tags assigned.
B. XGBoost Anomaly Detection Results
The anomaly detection component was realized with the
help of the model XGBoost, which is supplemented by fea-
tures from the previously processed legal data. The model
was shown to have an overall accuracy of 0.9207 or around
92.07%. The model achieved a reported ROC-AUC of 0.9897,
indicating that the model discriminated between the two
classes well on the test data.
Additional data can be found in the classification report.
For the first class, the model accuracy was 0.90, precision
was 0.92, and F1 was 0.91. These were 0.94, 0.92 and 0.93
for the second class. The F1 scores, both macro and weighted
averages were established as 0.92. The evaluation was done
on 1829 samples of which 838 samples were from the first
class and 991 of the second class.
Based on the results of the evaluation, the anomaly detec-
tion component using the XGBoost model could distinguish
between two different types of anomalies in the evaluation
data; however, the interpretation of the results should be based
on the available data. The algorithm output gives information
about the patterns, which are associated with the given anoma-
lies, it can’t be called to proof.
C. R-GCN Conflict Graph Results
R-GCN was created to understand relationships among
entities in the conflict graph of law. It came to be known
as a graph-based approach as a law case is known to have
different defendants and claims whose relationships cannot be
conveyed properly through independent records.
R-GCN showed progressive improvement in training accu-
racy. At epoch 10, training accuracy was 61.3%. At epoch 20,
this figure was 77.5%. After epoch 30, the training accuracy
increased to 81.2%, and epoch 40 brought 87.5%. At epoch 50,
training accuracy was 86.2%, while the total loss was 0.2747.
Thus, R-GCN achieved a final training accuracy of 90.00%.
The improvement seen throughout the training epochs in-
dicates that the graph model learned relationships shown in
the conflict graph. This is important for the case of multi-
defendant claims, as the mentioned information can be shown
in a way of relations among the evidence claims and events,
rather than through independent entities.
At the same time, it should be pointed out that the 90.00%
is only the training accuracy, and it cannot be considered as a
separate test accuracy.

D. LegalBERT Contradiction Detection Results
LegalBERT is used as a contradiction identification module.
It was created to be used on legal texts and to cross-reference
statements that could present conflicting information.
The LegalBERT module augments the job of entity recogni-
tion performed by BiLSTM-CRF. The NER model recognizes
the entities in the legal text and the contradiction model on
top of the language model examines the semantic relationships
between the statements.
Note the distinction as there can be two statements with the
same entities describing different events, dates, situations. The
number 75.00% should be considered as the current training
accuracy of the module constructed. The module needs further
testing on an independent validation or test set to conclude the
general applicability.

G. Overall Discussion
The results of the current research prove the effectiveness of
the developed framework in terms of the required calculating
elements for analyzing multifactorial and multiparty legal
judgments. First, the BiLSTM-CRF module detects tokens
with decent precision, reaching 93.16% in exact identification.
Second, the task of identifying anomalies using the XGBoost
classifier also demonstrates excellent results, with a precision
of 92.07% and a ROC AUC of 0.9897. Third, the R-GCN
structure managed to achieve a precision of 90% at the end of
the learning epoch. Finally, the LegalBERT structure reached
a precision of 75% after learning.
The current findings highlight the importance of applying
multiple classification modules rather than relying on the
result of a single classifier. Indeed, the task of processing
legal documents involves a variety of interrelated but different
activities. Whereas for detecting legal entities, the system
performs the operation of recognizing language constructs at
the level of sequences, the anomaly detection module works
with structured data features. The relation discovery procedure
employs the logical assumptions of the reasoning module,
while the contradiction resolution mechanism compares the
semantics of the processed tokens. Altogether, these operations
highlight the multifaceted nature of the framework, which
approaches the problem from multiple angles.
Finally, the current findings demonstrate that the developed
framework is a conceptual prototype that is assembled from
different modules rather than a unified whole. Indeed, as
mentioned by the authors in the concluding section, the
process of reporting the final decision along with the detailed
justification is completed in several parts. Altogether, the
research highlights the progress made in developing individual
artificial intelligence components but also demonstrates the
ongoing work in assembling the entire system, verifying and
validating the framework as a whole.
